# Taxes: what Sweden collects, and a calculator for your own tax

The site's tax page (`#taxes`) has three parts, each from an official source.

| Part | Source | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Tax revenue per tax type, Sweden and every OECD country | OECD Revenue Statistics (general government) | 1965 to the latest year, share of GDP and national currency |
| Tax on a salary by country, household and wage level | OECD Taxing Wages | 2000 to the latest year |
| Local income tax, burial and church fee per parish | Skatteverket open data | every year Skatteverket publishes |
| The rules of the calculator | Skatteverket, SKV 433 and *Belopp och procentsatser* | income year 2026 |

## The calculator

`frontend/src/taxes/calculator.ts` is the one implementation. The site uses it, and
`frontend/tests/unit/tax-calculator.test.ts` tests it. The rules for each year are in
`frontend/src/taxes/rules.ts`, with a source for every amount.

**What it computes**
- Final annual tax on:
  - salary
  - pension
  - other pension-qualifying benefits (unemployment, parental and sickness benefit)
  - sickness and activity compensation
  - the profit of a sole proprietorship, after self-employment contributions
  - interest and dividends
  - capital gains and losses
  - ISK and capital insurance (deemed income)
  - interest paid, e.g. a mortgage (the capital-deficit reduction)
  - the property fee of a small house
- The basic allowance, including the raised allowance from 66.
- The in-work tax credit, for people under and over 66.
- The pension contribution and its reduction.
- The earned-income reduction and the public service fee.
- What the employer pays on top, with the reduced rates by age and the youth reduction of 2026, and the resulting tax wedge.

**What it leaves out, and says so on the page**
- VAT and excise duties.
- Deductions (travel to work, double housing).
- The property-fee cap for pensioners.
- Foreign income.
- Tax-free benefits such as child benefit.

**Tested against Skatteverket.** Every row of the 2026 monthly withholding tables — tables 29–42, all six columns, 40,404 cases — gives the same tax to the krona. So do the worked examples in SKV 433. The table fixture is regenerated by `export_taxes.py`.

**A new income year.** Add its entry to `rules.ts` from Skatteverket's SKV 433 and *Belopp och procentsatser*. Then run `npm run taxes`, which fetches the new year's tables, and run `npm run test:unit`. The test fails until the rules match the tables.

## Reading the comparisons

- The **tax wedge** is income tax plus employee and employer contributions, less cash benefits, as a share of labour cost.
  - The OECD computes it at 67, 100 and 167 % of each country's own average wage.
  - Countries are therefore compared at the same place in their own wage scale, not at the same salary in kronor.
- **Revenue as a share of GDP** is tax *collected*, not tax *rates*.
  - It depends on what the tax pays for: pensions and health care are tax-financed in Sweden and partly private elsewhere.
  - It also depends on the size of the economy.

## Running

```bash
npm run taxes        # fetch, dbt build (tag:taxes), export
npm run test:unit    # the calculator against Skatteverket's tables
```
